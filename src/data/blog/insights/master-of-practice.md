There is a specific kind of wisdom that isn't found in books or workshops. It is a wisdom built through the body—a kind of mental resilience that comes from the simple, repetitive discipline of physical activity. Whether it is walking, yoga, or even just the mindful act of cleaning an apartment, these "gentle" resistances are often the only things that keep us grounded when the world starts to fall apart.

I experienced this firsthand in October 2023. At the time, my life was in a state of deep turbulence—an existential crisis compounded by the illness of a family member. Everything felt fragile. It was during one of those dark moments, sitting at my writing desk, that a video popped up in front of me:

🔗 [Shi Heng Yi – Der selbstbeherrschte Shaolin](https://www.srf.ch/play/tv/sternstunde-religion/video/shi-heng-yi---der-selbstbeherrschte-shaolin?urn=urn:srf:video:13a62e56-53d5-40e9-9cf7-3054dcf82f63)

I was hooked within twenty seconds of listening to Shi Heng Yi. It wasn’t just what he was saying; it was **the state he radiated—a profound, quiet peace** that I hadn't felt in a long time. I stopped working, lay down, and simply listened.

Shi Heng Yi’s thoughts seemed to silence the noise in my mind and relax my body. In that moment, something shifted. Despite the external chaos, I found a sense of internal peace. It became clear to me that I was on the right track with my own self-growth. Interestingly, he concluded the talk by suggesting a **ten-minute breathing exercise**—a practice I had already been applying for years. It was a moment of deep "alignment."

&nbsp;

#### The Message of Practice

The core of Shi Heng Yi’s message is constant: **Train the mind and the body. Practice, practice, and practice again.**

In a world filled with "hacks" and "masterclasses," his philosophy is refreshingly simple. Self-mastery isn't about attending another workshop or reading the next bestseller; it is about the quiet, daily commitment to your exercises. It is about the "**here and now**."
