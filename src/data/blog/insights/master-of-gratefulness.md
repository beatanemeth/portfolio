Sometimes, life brings us into contact with certain teachers at the exact moment we are ready to receive them. **Brother David Steindl-Rast** showed me the orientation of the heart—the art of living in deep, quiet alignment with the present moment.

&nbsp;

#### Gratefulness — The Art of Being Surprised

Brother David has "_surprised_" me twice in my life. The first time was in the spring of 2024. I have a personal rule that I only listen to podcast interviews if the title and the speaker resonate with me in that precise moment. As I was scrolling, a conversation with Brother David popped up on SRF:

🔗 [100 Jahre David Steindl-Rast – Nur im aktuellen Augenblick kann man wirklich glücklich sein](https://www.srf.ch/kultur/gesellschaft-religion/100-jahre-david-steindl-rast-nur-im-aktuellen-augenblick-kann-man-wirklich-gluecklich-sein)

Listening to him, I was immediately moved by his profound wisdom, reverence, and clarity. I felt an instant sense of gratitude, connection, and humility. What impressed me most was his deep humanity combined with a living, wise, and delightfully humorous presence.

His perspective on living gratefully caused me to re-evaluate my own relationship with gratitude entirely. Years earlier, I had heard about the self-help habit of keeping "gratitude journals" or constantly saying "thank you". Some of my friends who were interested in coaching were almost obsessed with it. Yet, I could never quite connect with the way pop-psychology books framed it or the atmosphere around that community.

Brother David’s understanding of gratitude was completely different. It wasn't transactional or superficial; it was an authentic posture towards life. And as someone who values **practice higher than theory**, I realized that the only secret is simply to practice it. Reading more books on the subject wouldn't help beyond a certain point—for me, the Kaizen approach of small, daily practice was far more important.

The second time Brother David crossed my path was in the spring of 2026. Another interview appeared, introducing me to his pioneering work in interreligious dialogue.

At the time, a debate had arisen within my circle of acquaintances about whether traditional Christianity could coexist with spiritual practices like Zen or Buddhism. Brother David’s open-hearted reflections offered me a clear, illuminating perspective—bridging traditions by looking beyond dogma to the shared essence of human experience.

#### The Essence of Grateful Living

At the core of Brother David's teaching is a quiet distinction between _gratitude_ and _gratefulness_.

Ordinary gratitude depends on receiving something specific; it rises and falls with external circumstances. Someone gives us a gift, we say thank you, and the feeling eventually fades. **Gratefulness**, however, does not wait for a specific reason. It is an ongoing awareness of the "gift-character" of existence itself—the recognition that every single moment is given. It is literally a state of **"great fullness"** that arises when we are awake to the present.

&nbsp;

> "Life is given to us; every moment is given. The only appropriate response therefore is gratefulness."
> _(The Music of Silence by Brother David)_

&nbsp;

Crucially, Brother David reminds us that we cannot be grateful _for_ everything. No one expects us to be grateful for war, illness, or painful loss. But we can be grateful **in** every moment—because every moment offers an opportunity. Even in moments of suffering, there is an opportunity to learn, to grow, or to stand compassionately with others.

&nbsp;

To turn this philosophy into action, he offers a remarkably simple three-step practice:

1. **Stop:** Pause long enough to notice the moment and break the momentum of reactivity.
2. **Look:** Open your senses to perceive the unique opportunity this moment holds.
3. **Go:** Act on that opportunity with presence and intention.

For Brother David, spirituality isn't an abstract religious concept; it is **aliveness on all levels**. Rooted in the Latin _spiritus_ (meaning "breath" or "life"), spirituality is simply the degree to which we are truly awake and alive in the here and now.
